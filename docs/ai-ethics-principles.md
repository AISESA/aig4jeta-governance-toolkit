# AI Ethics Principles for Just Energy Transitions in Africa – Overview

These **AI Ethics Principles** are designed to guide the responsible use of Artificial Intelligence in Africa’s energy sector, with a focus on **just and equitable energy transitions**.

They reflect African values such as:

- **Communal well-being and solidarity**  
- **Self-determination and agency**  
- **Respect for people and the environment**

This page offers a **plain-language overview**. The full, detailed version is maintained in:

- 👉 [`governance/ai-ethics-principles-v0.1.md`](../governance/ai-ethics-principles-v0.1.md)

---

## 1. Energy Justice & Equity

**Core idea:** AI in energy should **reduce**, not deepen, inequalities.

Questions to ask:

- Does this AI project help people who currently lack reliable or affordable energy?  
- Could it unintentionally shift costs or risks onto poorer households, rural areas, or marginalized groups?  
- Are benefits (e.g., better reliability, lower costs) fairly shared?

---

## 2. Human-Centricity & Well-being

**Core idea:** AI systems should **serve people**, not replace their judgment or undermine their rights.

Questions to ask:

- Are humans still in control of high-stakes decisions (e.g., disconnections, safety operations, tariffs)?  
- Have we considered impacts on workers and livelihoods in the energy sector?  
- Are there clear safety measures and fallback procedures if the AI fails?

---

## 3. Transparency & Explainability

**Core idea:** People impacted by AI should be able to **understand what it does and why**.

Questions to ask:

- Can we explain, in simple language, what data is used and what the system is doing?  
- Are regulators, operators, and communities able to question and challenge AI-supported decisions?  
- Is there documentation of data sources, model design, and known limitations?

---

## 4. Accountability & Governance

**Core idea:** There must always be a **clear line of responsibility**.

Questions to ask:

- Who is accountable if something goes wrong (an outage, unfair tariff, privacy breach)?  
- Are there clear policies, processes, and oversight bodies for AI in the energy institution?  
- Is there a way for communities or individuals to **raise complaints and seek redress**?

---

## 5. Data Privacy & Security

**Core idea:** AI should **respect privacy** and protect sensitive information.

Questions to ask:

- Are we collecting only the data we really need?  
- Do we have consent for the use of personal or community-level data?  
- Are there strong protections against data breaches, tampering, or misuse?

---

## 6. Local Relevance & Contextualization

**Core idea:** AI must be grounded in **African realities**, not just imported from elsewhere.

Questions to ask:

- Is the system trained and tested on data that reflects local conditions and diversity?  
- Are local experts, communities, and institutions involved in design and validation?  
- Does the system respect local cultures, norms, and knowledge?

---

## 7. Sustainability & Environmental Stewardship

**Core idea:** AI should help protect the environment and support **sustainable energy futures**.

Questions to ask:

- Does this project reduce emissions, improve efficiency, or support climate resilience?  
- Have we considered the energy use and hardware footprint of the AI itself?  
- Does it promote responsible use of natural resources and avoid environmental harm?

---

## How to Use These Principles

In practice, these principles can be used to:

- **Screen new projects** – as part of concept notes or funding proposals.  
- **Guide design and procurement** – adding ethics and governance criteria to TORs and contracts.  
- **Inform reviews and audits** – checking whether deployed systems still align with values and commitments.

They are closely linked with:

- The **TRUST Matrix** (which operationalizes many of these questions into a structured checklist).  
- The **Use Case Library**, where each documented use case shows how it aligns with these principles in practice.

## Living Principles

These AI Ethics Principles are **versioned** (starting with v0.1) and will evolve as:

- More African institutions implement AI in energy, and  
- We learn from both successes and failures.
